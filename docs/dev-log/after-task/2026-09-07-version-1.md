## 3a. Decisions and Rejected Alternatives

**The refutation target.** Four candidates were on the table: a cold double render of one chapter,
a leak grep of the live Pages HTML, an independent recompute of numbers a reviewer claimed
reproduce, and a re-run of the machine checks. **Rejected** the cold double render as the primary
probe: `_freeze` is shared with concurrent lanes and a mid-flight `mv` of a freeze directory is
exactly how an earlier draft of `tools/check-freeze.sh` destroyed a committed freeze (its own header
records this). Ran **all three of the others** instead; `tools/check-freeze.sh` supplies the
double-render evidence non-destructively, by re-rendering every input from the committed cache into
a scratch directory and diffing against `docs/site/`.

**Recomputed chapter 10 rather than chapter 1.** Chapter 1's reviewer had already re-derived its
numbers from the CSV in a standalone script, so repeating it tests little. Chapter 10's pedigree
counts are (a) the newest, (b) the argument the chapter's whole verdict rests on, and (c) pure
combinatorics over the raw file, so an independent implementation is genuinely independent. Did
chapter 1's wing statistics as well, as a second point.

**Fixed the scrub leak; did not fix the figure captions or the notebooks.** The leak is in
`docs/dev-log/`, inside this closer's write scope, and is a privacy defect — Rose's rule is to fix
the class, not file it. The caption and notebook defects are in `book/**` and `notebooks/**`, which
this close does not own; they are recorded as blocking residuals for the next lane instead of
silently repaired by an agent with no mandate over the chapters.

**Did not tick `G-scrub` in `.unlazy/stats-hours/GATES.md`.** The gate now passes, but the ledger is
out of this close's write scope and a closer marking his own gate green is the failure the
own-the-verifier principle exists to prevent. Reported, not ticked.

**Did not push, merge, or run any step of `tools/recreate-public.sh`.** Publication is the author's,
and the public-visibility recommendation below is negative in any case.

    and it is a limit: a defect that lives *inside* DRM.jl and is faithfully frozen would not be
    caught by anything in section 5.

## 11. Team Learning

**A corrected beat has three homes, and so does an uncorrected one.** The convention added at
`cffbf04` says a correction must land in the dialogue, the Summary and the Exercises. R3 is the same
lesson pointing the other way: chapter 3's review required *a* figure to get a caption, the fix gave
*that* figure a caption, and the other twenty went out bare. The rule generalises past corrections —
**when a review names an instance, the reviewer has told you the class**.

**Reviewers' independent refits corrected the lane's own engine issues.** Two of the seven issues
filed today, DRM.jl **#761** and **#762**, carry follow-up comments retracting or narrowing the
original filing after a reviewer refitted the model independently and got a different answer. #762 in
particular turned from "singular Σ at a far origin" into "a conditioning failure in one optimiser —
det Σ is invariant under a shear, so Σ is positive definite even at zero grams". A bug report is a
claim about someone else's software, and it deserves the same adversarial pass a chapter gets.

**Look at the output, and let the picture outrank the check.** Three source-level checks agreed that
chapter 10's figures were fine. One screenshot did not. The repo already had this written down after
the four-speeches CSS-grid failure; it recurred anyway, in a different disguise, on the same day the
whole-book pass ran a 13-page × 3-setting visual sweep — because that sweep was looking for layout
defects and a missing caption looks like a design choice.

**A gate that passes is not a gate that ran.** `scrub-check.sh` had been failing for over two hours
and nobody re-ran it; the summary handed to this close still said `SCRUB-OK`. **Re-run the check, do
not quote the last run of it** — especially a check whose whole purpose is privacy, and especially
when commits have landed since.

**Render collisions between concurrent writers are structural, not incidental.** Both
`tools/check-freeze.sh` and the chapter G5 gates manipulate `_freeze/` and `docs/site/`; two lanes
rendering at once, or a gate re-verifying while a writer renders, can destroy a committed freeze.
Today's mitigation was social — "re-render one file at a time", named in two commit messages
(`3ba412b`, `fc222ce`). It should be mechanical: a lock, or a scratch output directory for every
verification render, as `check-freeze.sh` already does and the gates do not.

**A stale `.git/index.lock` from GitHub Desktop** blocks every commit in the repository while
leaving no git process behind. The remedy is to wait 30 s, confirm no git process is running, and
only then remove it — and never remove one belonging to an in-flight operation. The handoff gate
reports such a lock and deliberately refuses to remove it, which is the right default for a tool and
the wrong default for a human who is blocked.

**Batch merges through a worktree.** Merging the day's chapter commits one at a time into `main`
from the shared checkout puts the working tree on `main` while other lanes are writing on the branch.
Doing the merges in a throwaway worktree keeps the shared checkout on its branch throughout.
