## 8. Was the fan-out worth it

Yes, on the numbers above, with one qualification. Wall-clock dropped from a 30–40 lane-hour /
week-of-sessions estimate to 6.5 hours in one sitting, and the plan's own revised bands (20–25 down to
2–3 lane-hours) tracked the actual finish closely from the 08:47 checkpoint onward — the estimate
machinery, once it saw the fan-out, was not fooling itself. But the fan-out bought concurrency, not
quality-for-free: six of the eight chapters reviewed today were blocked at least once (75%), three of
those needed three review rounds each (chapters 5, 7, 10), and the same four blocker classes —
mismatched nulls, an accepted null called "no," engine labels that oversell the engine, and a fix that
lands in dialogue but not the Exercises — recurred across chapters written by different sub-agents in
the same fan-out wave, which is what a shared review discipline (the stats-reviewer + fixer pairing)
is for and exactly what it caught. The fan-out is worth repeating; the review loop it depends on is not
optional overhead on top of it, it is the reason the parallel chapters converged on the same standard
the sequential ones did.
